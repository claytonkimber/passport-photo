How it works

  1. Pick your passport country. The guides, crop, background color and checks follow that country's rules.
  2. Line up with the guides. Live checks cover size, centering, head tilt, a neutral face, open eyes, even lighting and a plain background. It takes the photo for you once everything passes.
  3. Save or print. Get a JPEG at the official size for online applications, or a 4×6 in (10×15 cm) sheet to print at any photo kiosk.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. There are no ads, no account and no paid download. If it saves you a trip to a photo shop, you can chip in, but you don't have to.

Will the passport office accept my photo?

The app follows each country's published rules and checks the finished photo, but only the passport office decides. Look over the photo yourself before you submit it, and see the disclaimer.

Why isn't my country listed?

A country is listed only if its rules were confirmed on official government pages and it accepts photos you take yourself. Some countries photograph you at the passport office or require a certified photographer, so a home photo can't be used there. For other countries, the app offers the international ICAO standard (35×45 mm) that most passports follow; check your country's rules before using it.

Can I take it as a selfie?

You can, but it's better to have someone else take it with the back camera from about 1.2 m (4 ft) away. Arm's-length selfies can distort your face, and some countries, such as New Zealand, don't accept selfies.

What about glasses, hats and headphones?

Most countries ask you to remove glasses and anything covering your face or hair. The app can't check for these, so remove them before you start.

Does it work on iPhone?

It's built for current browsers on both Android and iPhone, and you can add it to your home screen from Safari. If your browser can't switch on the phone's flash, the Light button turns the screen white to light your face instead. If something doesn't work on your phone, please report it.
